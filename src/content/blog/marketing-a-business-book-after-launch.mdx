Most business books get a launch, a few weeks of attention, and then a place on the shelf. Melanie Johnson thinks that's where most of their value gets lost.

Melanie co-founded Elite Online Publishing, which has published over 3,000 books. Before that, she was a TV news anchor and helped run a television station in Dallas. She's even written a book on this topic, *Writing a Book to Build Your Business*. I asked her what a business book actually does when it works, why some launches fall flat, and how to keep a book working for years after it comes out.

## Don't let your book sit on the shelf

Melanie's short answer to what a book does for a business: whatever you keep using it for.

> "Don't let your book just sit there on the shelf. Then it's a real disservice to you."

She says there are a hundred ways to use a book. She covered three.

### Make it the first step for new prospects

Some authors give the book away free in exchange for shipping, often with bonuses you can't get on Amazon, and advertise that offer. Anyone who takes it is interested in your topic and in you, and now you know who they are. She mentioned Russell Brunson and Grant Cardone as examples.

### Use it to get a foot in the door

This is the one I'd underline. Say you're a consultant trying to reach a client who isn't responding. Your emails go to spam and you don't have their number. Send the book.

> "A secretary is not going to open an Amazon package to somebody. They're going to put it on their boss's desk, and they're going to open it."

Better still, send it directly with a personal note: open to page 55, there's something there that could change your business, and here's how to reach me if you'd like to talk. Curiosity does the rest. For more impact, send a copy to their whole leadership team. When everyone in the office has your book, they start talking about you and looking you up.

Melanie's company does this itself. Every month, they send 50 books to a dream list of ideal clients. They use AI to research who's on it and find the business addresses, which are much easier to get than phone numbers or emails. She says the response has been great.

### Turn it into years of content

A finished book is a stockpile of material: quotes, blog posts, social posts, videos, even a course. With AI to help, she says, one book can become thousands of pieces of content, enough for you or your team to use for years.

> "No matter how long you have your book, it could be five years, you still need to be posting about it. You still need to be talking about it. You still need to be sending it out to people."

## Why some book launches fall flat

When I asked why launches fail, Melanie's answer was one word: strategy. Then she told the story of an author with every advantage. The author had a reality TV profile, a husband who leads a church with 30,000 members, a traditional publisher, and an appearance on the Today Show.

The book didn't come close to a bestseller list.

The publisher did what traditional publishers usually do: push the hardback and pre-sales, then try to flood the market at release. There was plenty of PR, but no real plan. Melanie's team came in with what she calls a value-add strategy: offering the author's audience something of value to drive sales in a set window, plus Elite's own audience behind it. The book became an international bestseller in several categories and went on to win book awards. Her husband hired them for his next book.

Melanie's philosophy is to score on the first play, then keep the ball at that end of the field. A strong launch puts your book on the first page next to the best-sellers, where readers can actually see it. A book ranked a millionth gets no eyeballs. Then you keep the algorithm working by posting, doing podcast interviews, emailing your list, and gathering reviews. ([Amazon ads](/blog/amazon-ads-for-business-books) can help keep it visible too.)

## Hire a general contractor, or assemble the trades yourself?

Melanie compares publishing to renovating a house. You could learn electrical work from YouTube, but do you want to spend the time, and risk getting electrocuted? You could hire the cheapest electrician, and wonder why it doesn't look right.

She learned this firsthand. On a large house she managed herself, the finish was top quality. On a later remodel, she let someone else run it, paid good money, and got what she calls "apartment grade" work.

Authors do the same thing when they hire a cover designer here and a formatter there and try to stitch the trades together themselves. Her advice: if you hire help, look at their finished work first, and be careful. There are far more people offering to publish books than there used to be, and not all of them know what they're doing.

## What's changed in publishing in ten years

A lot, according to Melanie. There are millions more books on Amazon, so competition is much stiffer, and the algorithms keep changing. Recently, Amazon took down thousands of books at once and banned some authors from publishing; her team wrote the appeals that got their authors' books back up.

AI is the other big change. Amazon now asks authors whether AI wrote all or part of the book, or helped with editing. It doesn't penalize you for saying yes, at least for now, but nobody knows what it will do with that information later.

## Why AI-drafted manuscripts lose their stories

Melanie's team can usually spot a manuscript written mostly by AI. There are no stories in it, just short, snappy sentences and dashes, with no depth.

> "In an email, maybe that's okay. People want it short and snappy. But for a book, you want to feel depth and wisdom in there."

She still recommends working with a human editor, book coach, or [ghostwriter](/blog/hiring-a-ghostwriter-for-a-business-book). And the stories don't all have to be yours. In *The Science of Scaling*, she estimates 70% of the stories are about other people, many of them famous, and they still make the points land.

She also shared how she and her business partner used AI on their latest book, *Be Recognized*. They uploaded a large amount of their own material: YouTube videos, interviews, blog posts, emails, and past books. They gave it detailed instructions and recorded some new conversations for it. She used to describe the split as 10% you, 80% AI, 10% you. She's taken that back. It ended up closer to 10%, then 50% AI, then 40% of their own work at the end, mostly putting the stories back in. The result sounded like them.

## The one thing to get right on a first business book

Make sure readers come away seeing you as the expert. They should get to know you, your background, and how you got where you are, and they should see your authority on every page.

To plan it, Melanie uses what she calls the 10x10x3 method:

1. **Write down the 10 questions you're asked all the time:** on your website, on calls, at dinner parties.
2. **Then write the 10 questions people should ask you**, if they knew what you know.
3. **Answer each one, with a story that illustrates it.** Not just "here are three reasons"; give the story that shows why.

She calls this your "money book". Pick the subject you want to be talking about for the next year. Then write the book that would make a reader finish it and think: I want to work with this person.

## FAQ

**How do you keep marketing a business book after launch?**
Keep putting it in front of people. Melanie's advice is to keep posting about it, keep doing podcast interviews, keep emailing your list, keep collecting reviews, and keep sending copies to the people you want to work with, even five years on.

**How can a consultant use a book to reach new clients?**
Mail it. An Amazon package gets put on the boss's desk and opened. Send it with a personal note pointing to a specific page, and for more impact, send copies to their whole leadership team. Melanie's own company sends 50 books a month to its dream list.

**Why do some book launches fail, even with a big audience?**
No strategy. One of Melanie's authors had a reality TV profile, a 30,000-member church audience, and a Today Show appearance, but her launch missed the bestseller lists. With a launch strategy built around adding value for her audience, she became an international bestseller in multiple categories.

## What this means if your book is already out

Launch day is the start, not the finish. If your book has been out a while and isn't doing much, it isn't too late: pick the 50 people you'd most like to work with and mail them a copy with a note.
